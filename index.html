Welcome to India’s National Parks

India proudly hosts over 106 National Parks, making it a global leader in wildlife conservation and natural heritage. These parks are spread across the country's vast and diverse landscape — from the snowy heights of the Himalayas to the tropical forests of the south.

The very first national park of India was the famous Jim Corbett National Park, established in 1936. The largest is Hemis National Park in Ladakh, a high-altitude sanctuary known for snow leopards.

Some iconic names include:

  • Gir – the only home of the Asiatic Lion
  • Kaziranga – famous for the one-horned rhinoceros
  • Kanha – inspiration behind The Jungle Book

India ranks 3rd globally in terms of number of national parks. These protected zones shelter tigers, elephants, leopards, rhinos, and other rare species — helping fight extinction and preserve natural balance.

National Parks are not just about wildlife — they are about balance, beauty, and the bond we share with nature. They help control deforestation, preserve biodiversity, and offer peace to both animals and people.

Kanchenjunga in Sikkim is known for alpine wildlife and snow leopards.
